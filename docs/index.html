Small experiments on how AI agents act on outdated information, and what stops them. So far: checking first isn’t enough, and neither is a quick re-check. The check and the action have to be one locked step.

Lead story · Experiment 001

Checking first wasn’t enough

An AI refund agent checked an order. Someone refunded it a moment later. The agent refunded it again.

Checking first, even when it was required, still let 1 of 6 wrong refunds through. Doing the check inside the refund step stopped all of them.

A tougher follow-up, with the change really happening at the same moment, showed that a quick re-check just before refunding still failed about 1 time in 6. Only a check and refund done as one locked step held up.

What didn’t improve: every method still turned away one customer who deserved a refund.

A small test of the safety net around an AI agent. A simple rule plays the agent.

About

I’m interested in where AI agents go wrong in ordinary ways: acting on old information, or doing something a moment after it stopped being allowed. Each experiment starts from a research paper, tests one idea, and puts the code, the raw results and the limits on GitHub.

Built with help from AI tools (ChatGPT, OpenAI Codex, Claude Code). I choose the questions and check the results.
