Read a paper. Ask one question. Test it.

AI Thinking Lab is a habit I’m building: turning AI research into small experiments, one at a time, and sharing what actually happened, including what didn’t work.

The habit

  1. ReadPick a research paper about AI and read it properly.
  2. AskFind one small question from it that I can actually test.
  3. TestBuild the smallest experiment that can answer it, and write down what I expect first.
  4. SharePublish the code, the raw results, and what the test does and doesn’t show.

Each entry is small, uses made-up data, and states its limits. New entries are added when they have code, results and a write-up.

Lessons so far

  • A check can go out of date before you act on it. The check and the action need to happen as one locked step. (001)
  • Measure safety and usefulness separately. The safest method gave no more fair credits than the least safe one. (001)

About

I’m doing this to understand AI systems by testing ideas myself instead of only reading about them. I’m most interested in where AI agents go wrong in ordinary software ways, like acting on old information.

Built with help from AI tools (ChatGPT, OpenAI Codex, Claude Code). I choose the questions and check the results.
